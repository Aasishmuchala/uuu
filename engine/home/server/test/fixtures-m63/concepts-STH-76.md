# Concepts: Five small habits that keep a workshop tidy and safe
## 1. Five small habits, one steadier workshop (40 characters)
angle: Open with one short line naming the idea, then give each of the five habits its own slide in the middle.
why: A reader who stops after the first slide still has the one idea, and each slide after it adds one habit to picture.

## 2. What a bench looks like before and after five small habits (58 characters)
angle: Put each habit's before-and-after comparison on one middle slide, five slides in total, with room left for an opening and one closing ask.
why: A single before-and-after picture lets a reader judge the difference for themselves, with no claim needed from us.

## 3. Walk the bench: five habits, in the order you would do them (59 characters)
angle: Tell it as a walk around the bench from the moment you arrive to the moment you leave, each stop one habit.
why: A clear sequence, stop by stop, makes the habits easy to picture doing, not just reading about.

## 4. One habit, start to finish: putting a tool back where it lives (62 characters)
angle: Go deep on a single habit across the whole carousel, slide by slide, rather than skimming all five.
why: A reader who only remembers one thing from a feed post is more likely to remember it if it was the only thing said.

## 5. Tidy is not the same as perfect: a myth worth correcting (56 characters)
angle: Open by naming the idea that a tidy workshop means a spotless one, then correct it slide by slide with the five habits.
why: If a reader holds the belief that tidy means spotless, seeing it corrected gives them a reason to keep reading past the first slide.
