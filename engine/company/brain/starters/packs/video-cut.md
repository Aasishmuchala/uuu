# Video cut

A video cut takes a recording and keeps the good parts. It removes long pauses and stumbles
so the finished video moves along and says the same thing.

## How it works

- The sound is turned into words with start and end times.
- The cuts are chosen between words, so a cut never lands inside a word.
- The kept parts are joined in their original order into a new video. The original file is
  never changed.
- A list of what was kept and what was dropped is written beside the video.

## Limits, in plain words

- The source clip may be up to five minutes long and one gigabyte in size.
- The finished video is at most two minutes long.
- No bigger than full HD: 1920 by 1080 pixels at the most, with both sides even numbers.
- At most 30 frames every second.
- The finished file is not larger than 300 megabytes.
- The work stops by itself if it runs more than ten minutes.
- If the clip would come out longer than two minutes, say so and ask which parts to keep.

## Shape

- Keep a short breath, about a third of a second, between sentences so it does not feel rushed.
- Drop silences longer than about a second and a half.
- Keep the first sentence and the last sentence unless the owner says otherwise.
- Never reorder the speech. This tool trims; it does not rewrite.

## What a good one has

- Speech that sounds natural, not chopped.
- Six still pictures spread across the video for the owner to look at before playing it.
- A plain note of how much time was removed.

## What to avoid

- No cutting inside a word or a sentence that is not finished.
- No removing something the speaker meant to say.
- No music or sound unless the owner supplied the file.
- No changes to the original recording.
