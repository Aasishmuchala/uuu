# Captions

Captions are the words of a video, shown as text at the bottom of the picture as they are
said. They help people watching without sound and people who find speech hard to follow.

## How they are made

- The words are taken from the sound of the clip by the speech tool on this Mac. Nothing is
  sent anywhere.
- Each word gets a start time and an end time. A caption line is a few words that share a
  moment.
- The captions are burned into a new copy of the video. The original is never changed.

## Limits, in plain words

- The source clip may be up to five minutes long and one gigabyte in size.
- The new video is at most two minutes long, no bigger than full HD (1920 by 1080), and at
  most 30 frames every second.
- The finished file is not larger than 300 megabytes.
- The work stops by itself if it runs more than ten minutes.

## Shape

- One or two lines at a time, each line at most about 40 characters.
- Each caption stays on screen long enough to be read, at least one second.
- Plain white type with a dark edge or a soft box behind, so it reads on any picture.
- Captions sit in the lower part of the picture and never cover a face or the logo.

## What a good one has

- The words match what is said, including names. Names the owner supplied are spelled as given.
- Breaks fall at the end of a thought, not in the middle of a phrase.
- A short list at the end of any word the tool was unsure of, so the owner can check.

## What to avoid

- No words the speaker did not say.
- No cutting a caption off in the middle of a word.
- No changes to the owner's original file.

## Before you hand it over

- Read it once more, start to finish, as the person who will receive it.
- Say plainly at the top if anything was unclear or missing.
- Mark every line that came from a guess, so the owner can check it.
