# Post

A post is a short piece of writing for a social page. It makes one point in plain words and
asks for at most one thing.

## Shape

1. **The hook**: the first line. It must work alone, because it is all a reader sees before
   deciding to read on. A question, a surprise or a plain useful fact.
2. **The point**: two to five short lines that make the one point.
3. **The ask**: one line saying what to do next, or none if the post only informs.

## Length

- Between 30 and 120 words for most pages.
- Short lines with a blank line between them. A phone screen is narrow.
- At most three tags, placed at the very end, only if the page uses them.

## What a good one has

- One point. If there are two, write two posts.
- Words a customer would use, not words we use inside the company.
- A reason to care in the first line.
- A voice that matches the Brand page: the same warmth, the same plainness.
- A picture or a carousel slide suggested when it would help, described in one line.

## What to avoid

- No claim on the never-say list.
- No price, discount or date unless the owner gave it for this post.
- No exaggeration, and no promise the support page does not make.
- No links to places we have not checked.
- No posting. Posts are drafts. The owner approves each one before anything goes out.

## Before you hand it over

- Read the first line alone. Would a stranger keep reading?
- Count the asks. There should be one at most.
- Read it out loud once. If you stumble, shorten the sentence.
- Check each word of the never-say list against the text.
- Say which source in the brain the post comes from, in one line, so the owner can check it.
