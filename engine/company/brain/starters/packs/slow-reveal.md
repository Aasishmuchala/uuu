# Slow reveal

A slow reveal is a short video made from still pictures. Each picture drifts or zooms
gently, one fades into the next, and a few words may appear over a picture.

## Parts

- A list of pictures, in order. Each has a length in seconds and one move: slowly in, slowly
  out, drifting left, drifting right or still.
- An optional caption for each picture, a few words.
- A gentle fade between pictures.

## Limits, in plain words

- The finished video is at most two minutes long. Most reveals work best at fifteen to forty
  seconds.
- No bigger than full HD: 1920 by 1080 pixels at the most, with both sides even numbers.
- At most 30 frames every second.
- The finished file is not larger than 300 megabytes.
- The work stops by itself if it runs more than ten minutes.
- Only pictures the owner supplied or that we drew may be used.

## Shape

- Between three and twelve pictures.
- Each picture is on screen for three to six seconds. Less than three feels hurried.
- Use one kind of move for most pictures, with a different one only to mark a change.
- A caption is at most eight words and stays on screen long enough to be read twice.
- Pictures that do not fit the frame are cropped from the centre. Check faces and text first.

## What a good one has

- A clear order: where we start, what changes, where we end.
- A first picture that makes sense on its own.
- Moves slow enough to feel calm, never dizzy.
- A still picture of the middle, so the owner can judge it without playing the video.

## What to avoid

- No picture we have no right to use.
- No captions that state a price, date or promise the owner did not give.
- No claim on the never-say list.
- No music unless the owner supplied a file.
